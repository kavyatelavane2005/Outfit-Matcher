How it works

  1. Find the garment

    Background removal cuts the photo down to the item, so shadows and hangers do not confuse later steps.

  2. Identify it

    A ResNet18, fine-tuned on about 9,000 catalogue photos, names one of 15 garment types. Below 55% confidence it asks you instead of guessing.

  3. Read the colour

    K-means clustering on the garment pixels finds the main colour, matched to a 25-colour fashion palette.

  4. Plan three looks

    Colour-theory rules pick a neutral pairing, a tone-on-tone look and a contrast look.

  5. Generate the pieces

    SD-Turbo draws each missing item. Every image is checked for its planned colour and for being the right kind of garment.

Concepts used

Transfer learning
The classifier starts from a network pretrained on ImageNet and only learns what is new, instead of training from scratch.
Two-phase fine-tuning
First only the new last layer is trained, then the last convolutional block is unfrozen at a lower learning rate. The best epoch on validation data is kept.
Data augmentation
Random crops, shifts, blur, flips and colour jitter make the model cope with photos unlike the catalogue.
Class-weighted loss
Rarer garment types count more, so T-shirts do not dominate.
Diffusion model
SD-Turbo starts from noise and removes it step by step, guided by a text prompt, to draw each product photo.
Classical vision
Cut-out (k-means background model and GrabCut), colour detection (k-means) and CIE Lab colour distance are not deep learning.

Limits

Rules, not taste
Pairings come from colour theory, not from a model trained on real outfits.
Small training photos
The classifier learned from tiny catalogue shots, so people wearing the item or cluttered scenes can be misread.
Generated, not real
The matching pieces are AI images, not products you can buy.
